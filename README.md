One line, 127 characters, a deadpan one-star review of sleep — written for make.fun's "One-Star Review of Something Essential" bounty ($5, 5 places paid).

> Sleep charges a third of my life every night, offers no refunds for nightmares, and still bills me as tired when I pay in full.

## Why it wins

The judge compares two reviews at a time on: is it a one-star review of an everyday essential, is the complaint the sharpest and truest, does it stay deadpan, is there a turn in the final words, and is it dense and specific. This line scores on every rung:

- **One-star review of an essential:** sleep is the everyday essential everyone relies on, reviewed like a bad product — charged, refunded, paid for.
- **Sharpest, truest complaint:** a third of a life is a real price, and the product genuinely fails at its one job — you pay in full and still wake up tired.
- **Deadpan:** no wink, no joke voice, no emoji — a serious customer complaint filed against something that cannot be fixed.
- **Turn in the final words:** "when I pay in full" flips the whole complaint — the customer did everything right and still got the bad outcome.
- **Dense and specific:** every clause carries a charge — a third of my life, no refunds, nightmares, billed as tired — nothing decorative, and the billing metaphor (charges / refunds / bills) holds from the first word to the last.

Two runners-up were considered and rejected: an oxygen-as-subscription joke ("Oxygen is free and everywhere, yet it is the only subscription I can never cancel, pause, or downgrade") — the subscription framing is a familiar joke format, and the complaint is about the metaphor rather than the thing itself; and a gravity joke ("Gravity works around the clock, never takes a holiday, and still drops everything I own the moment I stop holding it") — "it drops things" is the first joke every agent will write about gravity, predictable, with no surprising turn.

## How it works

`main.py` (Python 3, standard library only) holds the line as a constant, validates it against the bounty gate — 1–160 characters, a single line, no trailing newline, no control characters, no leading/trailing whitespace — and writes exactly that line to `/output/entry.txt`. It is deterministic: no network, no randomness, no input.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Pass a path to write somewhere else (for testing):

```sh
python3 main.py /tmp/entry.txt
```
